# Certified Red Team Professional
This is a review of my experience preparing for and taking the Certified Red Team Professional (CRTP) exam by Altered Security.

&nbsp;
## Exam Details
- Duration: 24 hours for the hands-on exam, plus 48 hours to submit the report.
- Format: **Practical**. No multiple-choice questions.
- Environment: Multi-host AD lab environment.
- Coverage
  - Enumeration
  - Local Privilege Escalation
  - Lateral Movement
  - Domain Privilege Escalation
  - Persistence
  - Bypassing Advanced Threat Analytics

&nbsp;
## Prerequisites
- Basic understanding of Active Directory structure and behavior
- Familiarity with Windows command line and PowerShell
- Exposure to tools such as:
  - BloodHound
  - PowerView
  - Rubeus
  - Mimikatz
- Experience with red team or pentest methodology is a plus

&nbsp;
## My Experience
I chose this certification after doing research and asking the community which training and certification would be best to start my offensive security journey. The majority recommended CRTP, so I decided to pursue it as my entry point.
My background is in defensive security, with a basic understanding of Active Directory and PowerShell command-line usage. Overall, the CRTP material was excellent, it covered a wide range of topics with clear explanations and included high-quality lab challenges.
The labs are based on Windows Server 2019 and simulate realistic Active Directory environments. I genuinely enjoyed working through the course content and completing each learning objective. Most of the techniques taught are highly applicable to real-world penetration testing and reflect what I’ve encountered during actual engagements.
This certification is highly recommended for beginner red teamers or anyone starting to explore Active Directory exploitation.
To pass the exam:
- Complete **all the challenges** in the lab.
- Read and practice **every section** of the CRTP lab manual.
- Take detailed notes, especially commands (Extremely helpful during the exam)

After completing and passing CRTP, my next goal is to take **CRTE** to build a deeper and more advanced understanding of AD exploitation techniques.
